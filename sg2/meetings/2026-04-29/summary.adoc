=== 2.6 Discussion: Document Scope and Minimum Requirements

Peter Thorne raised a concern that the team repeatedly revisits decisions that have already been made — in particular, the deliberate decision to restrict the document to minimum, essential components. He noted that as the team considers bringing in additional external reviewers, there is a real risk that those reviewers, without a clear briefing on past decisions, could pull the document in different directions. He suggested that the minimum scope remit should be re-stated at the start of each meeting.

Ian clarified the practical meaning of "minimum requirements" in this document: mandatory requirements are written as "shall" and represent the minimum set; non-mandatory guidance is written as "should" and may remain in the document to provide context, ideally linked back to the relevant "shall" requirement. The goal is not to cull all non-mandatory content, but to ensure that the "shall"/"should" distinction is applied carefully and consistently.

David confirmed this framing and noted the importance of the usability and maintainability requirements (requirement class 14) in ensuring that systems remain accessible and maintainable over time, including the ability to migrate data to a different system.

Peter reiterated that the document is effectively being written for smaller national meteorological and hydrological services (NMHS) that may have limited resources and technical capacity. Producing a complex, over-specified document risks discouraging exactly the members who most need CDMSs. He noted that larger, well-resourced services are probably already compliant; the document should be kept as simple as possible for those who are not.

Ian noted a complementary perspective: one of the medium-term goals of this work is to be able to assess whether a given software product conforms to the document's requirements, so that small countries can make an informed choice between available products rather than building their own. In that case, the document does not need to be actionable by a country building a CDMS themselves — it needs to be checkable.

Denis observed that even large services find CDMS development complex today, given the number of standards involved. He suggested the document's most useful function may be to push existing free and open-source CDMS products (such as Climsoft and MCH) to adopt and demonstrate conformance with the standard.

Dwayne confirmed that he had shared the document with climate personnel in several Caribbean countries (including Jamaica) for feedback and was checking requirements with those users in mind. Patrick noted he was reviewing the document in the context of African countries. William was reviewing with the 15+ Pacific Island countries and territories he works with in mind. Dwayne supported the minimum requirements approach but noted that developers of existing CDMS products being on the team is a strength: they are already checking their own systems against the requirements and flagging where the requirements may not be achievable.

David took an action to work with Ian to ensure clear communication of the document's scope — both internally at the start of future meetings and externally to any reviewers being brought in.

=== 2.7 Technical Discussion: Station Grouping

Denis suggested adding a requirement for the ability to group stations, which he described as a capability that saves significant operational effort. Station groups might be defined by administrative geography (country, region, department), observing network role (G-BON, urban, etc.), or other criteria. He noted this functionality is important for applying workflows and products at the group level rather than station by station.

David agreed and identified two distinct aspects: (a) the ability to assign a station to one or more named groups, and (b) the ability to define and apply workflows or products to a group of stations. He confirmed these would be added to the document.

=== 2.8 Discussion: Data Model and Non-proprietary Formats

Dwayne asked whether the document would require adherence to a specific data model (in particular, the WMO climate data model, CDM). David explained that requirement class 1 specifies that the system must implement a data model capable of storing the required data, and that the CDM will be included as an informative resource at the end of the document. The document aims to remain implementation-agnostic and will not mandate a specific data model.

Peter clarified that a data model is distinct from a data format and from how data is physically stored. The risk to avoid is data stored in proprietary formats that may not be readable in future decades. The document should recommend the use of non-proprietary, open formats.

Ian noted that the CDM work did recommend a specific exchange format to support interoperability, and that a larger discussion on the CDM may be warranted when the INFCOM submission document is available.

Patrick supported a platform-agnostic approach. He noted that the document already includes backup requirements referencing open standard formats, which partially addresses the lock-in risk.

Denis mentioned the MeteoGate project, a European initiative developing APIs for data exchange and linkage. David noted that this work has influenced discussions within the WMO SG-Fit group on APIs (led by Jeremy Tandy, UK Met Office), and the two streams are related.

David confirmed he would check whether the functional requirements already include a recommendation to use non-proprietary formats; if not, he would add it as a GitHub issue.
